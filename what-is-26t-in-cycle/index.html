What Does 26T Mean on a Cycle?

The term 26T is frequently used incorrectly in bicycle advertisements. It may refer to a 26-inch wheel bicycle, or it may correctly describe a chainring or sprocket with 26 teeth. These are completely different measurements.

Wheel size versus tooth count

For chainrings, cogs and sprockets, the letter T means teeth. A 26T chainring has twenty-six teeth. For complete bicycles, sellers sometimes write 26T when they actually mean 26-inch wheels.

Tyre sidewall markings such as 26 × 1.95 and an ETRTO number identify wheel and tyre compatibility more accurately than a marketplace title.

How gearing changes

A smaller front chainring produces an easier climbing gear but less distance per pedal revolution. A larger front chainring produces a harder, faster gear. At the rear cassette, the effect is reversed: a larger sprocket makes climbing easier.

Compatibility also depends on bolt-circle diameter, crank design, chain speed and derailleur capacity, so tooth count alone is not enough when ordering parts.

What to check before ordering

Photograph the existing markings, count mounting bolts, record the drivetrain speed and check the manufacturer specification. For tyres and tubes, copy the complete size printed on the tyre sidewall.
